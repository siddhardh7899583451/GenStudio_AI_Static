How it works

Each digit class has a learned mean pattern (template) and isotropic Gaussian noise. New samples are drawn as x ~ N(μ_digit, σ²I), clipped to a valid intensity range, and rendered as 8×8 pixel grids. A simple template-matching “judge” measures agreement — the same educational idea as training a class-conditional generative model and evaluating with an independent classifier.

Autoregressive Sequence Generation

Miniature language model: factorizes P(x) = ∏ P(xt | x<t) and samples one character at a time with temperature control.

Temperature & Sampling

              

                Temperature scales the logits before the softmax.
                Low T → peaked (more deterministic), High T → flatter (more creative / random).
                This is the same principle used in large language models when decoding.

Generative vs Discriminative

          

            Core ideas behind generative modeling, density estimation, and why joint structure matters.
          

        


        

          

            
G

            
Generative Models

            

              Learn the joint distribution P(X, Y) or the density P(X | Y).
              Once trained they can sample new data that looks like the training distribution.
              Examples: Gaussians, VAEs, diffusion models, autoregressive LMs.
            

          


          

            
D

            
Discriminative Models

            

              Learn the conditional P(Y | X) directly (decision boundaries).
              Excellent at classification, but they do not model how the data itself is distributed
              and cannot generate realistic new samples.
            

          


          

            
AR

            
Autoregressive Factorization

            

              Any joint can be written as a product of conditionals:
              P(x1…xT) = ∏ P(xt | x1…xt−1).
              Language models (and pixel-by-pixel image models) exploit this to generate sequences
              one token at a time.
            

          


          

            
Σ

            
Why Covariance Matters

            

              Independent feature models (product of marginals) miss correlations.
              A full covariance / joint generative model captures the “shape” of the data
              and produces more realistic samples. This is the structure & dependency principle.
